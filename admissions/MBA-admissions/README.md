Several of the best schools in the world are **not** Ivies: Stanford, Chicago Booth, Kellogg, MIT
Sloan, Berkeley Haas, LBS, INSEAD. The usual shorthand is **M7** (Harvard, Stanford, Wharton,
Booth, Kellogg, Columbia, MIT Sloan) and **T15/T20** in the US. The page covers all of these.

## 2. An honest read of your profile

| Item | How adcoms read it |
|---|---|
| IIT Bombay brand | Strong. Adcoms know IITB, the entrance exam, and its harsh grading. |
| CGPA 6.56 | **The weak point.** Below the ~7.5-8.5 most IITians at M7 schools have. It needs offsetting, not explaining away. |
| 12th 77.17% | Rarely looked at for MBA. Only a small factor in Indian schools (ISB / IIMs weight academics). |
| 10th 90.8% | Neutral to positive; hardly looked at. |
| Indian male engineer | The most over-represented applicant pool in the world. You compete against other IITians, so you need a **higher GMAT** and a **distinct story** than the class average suggests. |
| Work experience | **The biggest factor you still control.** Top schools average 4.5-5 years. |

**How to offset the 6.56:**

1. **A high GMAT, especially Quant.** It is the single best counter to a low GPA. Aim for a Quant section score of **85+**.
2. **An alternative transcript.** Take one or two graded quant courses and get an A: HBS Online CORe, UCLA Extension / UC Berkeley Extension calculus or statistics, or MITx MicroMasters (Supply Chain fits your IEOR minor).
3. **Rising grades.** If your last 2-3 semesters are 7.5+, say so in the optional essay ("upward trend").
4. **A short optional essay.** Three or four factual sentences on why the CGPA is low and what has changed since. No excuses.
5. **Work that shows numbers.** Promotions, ownership, measurable impact (₹, %, people led).

GMAT Focus Edition scores run **205-805**. Rough old-GMAT equivalents: 645 ≈ 700, 665 ≈ 720,
685 ≈ 740, 705 ≈ 760, 715 ≈ 770.

GRE is accepted everywhere listed here too. Rough targets: 330+ for the M7, Quant 168+.

## 4. Your best picks

### Among the Ivies
1. **Cornell Johnson.** The most realistic Ivy. The **1-year Tech MBA** at Cornell Tech (NYC) is built for engineers moving into product or tech. It costs about a year less and the Park Leadership Fellowship pays full tuition. A 685+ GMAT and 2-3 years of work make you competitive.
2. **Yale SOM: Silver Scholars.** The best Ivy route **while you are still at IITB**. It takes students straight from undergrad into a 3-year programme (year 1 MBA core, year 2 a full-time internship, year 3 electives). It wants a strong GMAT and maturity.
3. **Columbia.** A big class (~850), **January J-Term** entry, and a **Deferred Enrollment** programme for final-year students. A reach, but a broad one.
4. **Harvard 2+2, Wharton, Tuck.** Reach. Apply to HBS 2+2 this spring only if your GMAT is 715+.

## 6. How applications work (most schools)

1. **Test:** GMAT / GRE / EA. Score sent to the school.
2. **Online application:** resume (1 page), academic transcripts (IITB grade sheets; WES evaluation not usually needed), work history.
3. **Essays:** 1-3 essays (career goals, "why us", leadership). Some use video (Kellogg, Yale, INSEAD).
4. **Recommendations:** 1-3, ideally from current and former supervisors.
5. **Application fee:** $100-$275 (fee waivers exist via Forté, Consortium, school events).
6. **Interview:** invite only (~25-40% of applicants). In person, virtual, or with an alum. HBS asks for a short written reflection afterwards.
7. **Decision:** about 3 months after the round deadline. Waitlists are common.
8. **Deposit and visa:** ~$2k-$7k deposit, then an F-1 (US), Student (UK) or other visa. US schools are STEM-designated, which means 3 years of OPT work authorisation.

### Rounds and intakes, explained
- **US schools** have **one intake: Fall (Aug-Sep)**, with 2-3 application **rounds**: R1 (Sep-Oct), R2 (Jan), R3 (Mar-Apr). Internationals should apply in R1/R2, because R3 seats are few and visas take time.
- **"Spring" entry exists only at a few schools:** Columbia **J-Term** (starts January, 16 months, no summer internship), **INSEAD** (January and September), **HEC Paris** (January and September), ISB (April), IIM PGPX (April).
- **UK / Europe** run more rounds (4-5 stages) and mostly start in Sep.

**Where the 2026-27 cycle stands today (16 Oct 2026):** R1 deadlines for Fall 2027 entry (HBS,
Stanford, Wharton, Booth: Sept 2026) have passed. **Round 2 (early January 2027) is the open round.**
HBS R2 is 5 Jan 2027; Wharton, Booth, Kellogg, Columbia, Stanford R2 fall between early and mid January.

### How to pay the least
1. **Score high on the GMAT.** It drives merit money more than anything else. Every 20 points above the class average helps.
2. **Apply in Round 1.** Most scholarship money goes in R1.
3. **Apply to 2-3 schools a tier below your reach.** A big scholarship from Tepper, Ross or Cornell can beat a no-money M7 admit.
4. **Negotiate politely.** Booth, Kellogg, Ross, Darden, Fuqua, Tepper, Stern and Johnson will often reconsider an award if you show a better offer from a peer school.
5. **Consider 1-year programmes.** INSEAD, Oxford, Cambridge, ISB, Kellogg 1Y, Cornell Tech: half the opportunity cost.
6. **Earn during the MBA.** The US summer internship pays ~$35k-$50k; TA/RA roles and paid campus jobs help too.
7. **Employer sponsorship.** Consulting firms (MBB) and some Indian conglomerates sponsor MBAs in return for a service bond.
8. **Apply to Knight-Hennessy, Pershing Square and Chevening.** They are hard to get, but they pay the full cost.
